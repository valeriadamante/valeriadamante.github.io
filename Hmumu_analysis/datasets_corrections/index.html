    Standard corrections¶

    The correction inventory records the payload family, campaign and working points used for each data-taking period. It covers:

    • jet energy corrections and jet energy resolution;
    • pile-up weights and jet-veto maps;
    • muon identification, isolation and trigger scale factors;
    • muon momentum scale/resolution and FSR recovery;
    • b-tagging working points;
    • certified luminosity masks.

    Analysis selections¶

    This section separates the agreed prescription from the current implementation. Scale factors are evaluated on raw object quantities. Jet and MET selections use corrected quantities. The kinematic convention for muon and electron cuts is still to be decided; until that choice is frozen, both the current behavior and the open decision are stated explicitly below.

    Muon correction chain¶

    1. Input momentum choice. Both the standard NanoAOD momentum and the beam-spot-constrained (BSC) momentum are retained. For each muon, the BSC branch is selected when Muon_bsConstrainedChi2 < 30; otherwise the standard NanoAOD branch is used.
    2. ScaRe. The momentum scale correction is applied to data and MC; the resolution smearing is applied to MC. It is evaluated separately for the NanoAOD and BSC momentum branches, including the scale and resolution variations.
    3. FSR recovery. The selected FSR-photon four-vector is added after ScaRe. The nominal analysis branch is Muon_p4_FSR_corr (and its Muon_pt_FSR_corr projection); shifted branches repeat the construction for each ScaRe scale/resolution variation.
    4. Reconstructed quantities. ScaRe+FSR four-vectors are available for corrected muon ordering, $m_{\mu\mu}$, dimuon $p_T$ and the other dimuon observables. Whether the analysis $p_T$ cuts and ordering must use these corrected quantities or the raw quantities remains an explicit analysis choice.

    Open selection decision. A single convention must still be chosen for every muon and electron kinematic cut: either raw quantities or the relevant corrected quantities. This decision must cover object acceptance, ordering, trigger-leg thresholds, the 30 GeV low-$p_T$ working-point switch and the electron veto, as well as whether mass-region cuts use a raw or corrected dimuon four-vector. It must then be applied consistently to the nominal selection and its systematic variations.

    Current implementation. The skim-level good-muon mask and trigger-object matching currently use Muon_pt_raw_noCorr, before ScaRe and FSR. The corrected branch determines ordering, dimuon reconstruction and the downstream analysis thresholds. The electron veto currently uses raw NanoAOD electron kinematics. These are implementation facts, not yet the final analysis prescription.

    Muon scale factors: evaluation coordinates¶

    The agreed prescription is that ID, isolation and single-muon-trigger SFs are evaluated on raw muon kinematics. ScaRe and FSR must not change the coordinates passed to the correctionlib payload, and ScaRe variations must not cause the efficiency SFs to be recomputed. The SFs affect only MC weights and do not reject events.

    The current lookup receives:

    • $p_T$: mu{1,2}_pt_raw_noCorr, i.e. the BSC/NanoAOD choice before ScaRe and before FSR;
    • η: mu{1,2}_eta, currently taken from the nominal ScaRe+FSR four-vector;
    • the unmodified NanoAOD isolation and ID observables, trigger-match flag and event number required by the correction helper.

    Implementation mismatch to resolve: the SF lookup presently has raw $p_T$ but corrected+FSR η. To satisfy the prescription, η must also be read from the raw muon coordinates. The lookup is not recomputed under the ScaRe shape variations. The ID/ISO lookup is applied above its payload threshold (15 GeV for 2022–2023BPix and 10 GeV for 2024–2025); the trigger lookup requires $p_T>26$ GeV. Outside the valid selection the corresponding factor is unity.

    For 2022–2023BPix the nominal event weight uses Medium-ID and Loose-PF-isolation SFs for both muons. For 2024–2025 the selected SF working point is conditional: a muon below 30 GeV uses Tight-ID and Tight-PF-isolation SFs, while a higher-$p_T$ muon uses Medium-ID and Loose-PF-isolation SFs. Whether this working-point switch follows raw or corrected $p_T$ is part of the pending muon-selection decision. The event trigger factor is multiplied once, together with the two per-muon ID and isolation factors. Up/down ID, isolation and trigger variations replace only their corresponding factor. The current 2026 central weight is explicitly temporary and contains no muon ID, isolation or trigger SF because those columns are absent from the available 2026 DY skims.

    Mass regions and VBF definition¶

    • inclusive: $60<m_{\mu\mu}<150$ GeV;
    • Z sideband: $70<m_{\mu\mu}<110$ GeV;
    • signal fit: $115<m_{\mu\mu}<135$ GeV;
    • Higgs sidebands: 110–115 or 135–150 GeV;
    • VBF candidate: the selected jet pair with the largest $m_{jj}$, requiring $m_{jj}\geq400$ GeV and $|\Delta\eta_{jj}|\geq2.5$; the histogram-level VBF category additionally requires leading/subleading jet $p_T\geq35/25$ GeV.
