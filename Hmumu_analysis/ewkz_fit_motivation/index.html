    From the DNN fits to an EWK Z fit with the VBF selector¶

    2026-10-02. Why the H->mumu VBF fit needs a measured EWK Z normalisation, and why that measurement is done in the dimuon sidebands with the VBF selector. Every number below is an expected significance on the pre-fit Asimov (r = 1), 7 eras (2022-2026), DNN DNN_NNOutput in the VBF category, unless stated otherwise. Sources at the end.

    1. The DNN fit, region by region¶

    The signal fit uses the SR (Signal_Fit_VBF, 115-135 GeV) and optionally the H sideband (H_sideband_VBF, 110-115 and 135-150 GeV), with three ways of treating the DY jet components (Hard / one pile-up jet / two pile-up jets):

    DY model SR only SR + H sideband SR only, DY scales shape-only SR + H sideband, DY scales shape-only
    lnn (Hard, PU1, PU2 constrained by the 012J fit) 2.00 2.13 2.07 2.17
    rateparam-pu (PU free) 1.92 2.09 2.01 2.13
    rateparam-both (PU and Hard free) 1.64 1.99 1.64 1.99
    inclusive (one DY template, free) – 2.03 – –

    Stat only (every constrained nuisance frozen): 2.51 for lnn. Three things are visible:

    • The H sideband helps exactly as much as the DY is left free: +6% with lnn, +9% with rateparam-pu, +21% with rateparam-both. It constrains the DY, which is the same process in SR and sideband.
    • Treating the DY QCD scales as shape-only (their normalisation already comes from the 012J fit in the Z sideband) is worth +2-4%.
    • The systematics cost ~15% (2.17 against 2.51). The freeze breakdown of the best card (SR + H sideband, shape-only, lnn) says where:
    frozen significance
    nothing 2.17
    theory (QCD scales, PDF, parton shower) 2.31
    QCD scales of V processes (DY and EWK Z) 2.28
    MC statistics 2.28
    JES + JER 2.19
    luminosity 2.19
    DY normalisations, muons, pile-up 2.17 each

    The largest single impact on r is QCD_fac_scale_V (±0.13), ahead of the MC statistics of the last DNN bins, JER and luminosity.

    2. What the DNN fit assumes about EWK Z¶

    In the three highest DNN bins of the SR, where the sensitivity is, the background is made of DY and EWK Z (pre-fit, lnn):

    era EWK Z / background DY / background EWK Z / signal
    2022 0.28 0.61 1.5
    2022EE 0.29 0.58 1.6
    2023 0.21 0.68 1.6
    2023BPix 0.23 0.67 1.6
    2024 0.22 0.69 1.6
    2025 0.19 0.68 1.7
    2026 0.36 0.54 1.5

    EWK Z is a fifth to a third of the background and about 1.6 times the signal. Yet in every DNN card its normalisation is constrained only by the generator integration error of the cross section (±0.5%), plus the QCD-scale and parton-shower shapes. The fit treats the EWK Z yield as known.

    How much that matters (SR + H sideband, lnn, high-score SR in the fit):

    EWK Z normalisation significance change
    as in the cards (±0.5%) 2.14 –
    lnN 10% 2.07 −3%
    lnN 30% 1.86 −13%
    free (rateParam common to SR and H sideband) 1.63 −23%

    So the sensitivity rests on a number that is not measured. Note the last row: the H sideband is in that fit, and still the DNN fit cannot tell EWK Z from DY on its own. The DNN separates H from background, not EWK Z from DY.

    3. Why the EWK Z normalisation is not "known"¶

    • The EWK Z prediction (madgraph, herwig/pythia showers, generated with m(jj) > 120 GeV at LHE) has never been checked on data in this phase space. The generator cut itself costs little (0.75% of the EWK Z yield in the VBF category sits at 120 < m(jj, LHE) < 150 GeV, with a <1% effect on the DNN shape: results/ewk_lhe_mjj/).
    • The pre-fit data/MC ratio in the sidebands follows the VBF-selector score, differently in each era (figure prefit_ratio_by_era): in the Z sideband it falls from 1.05 to ~0.90 in 2022 and 2022EE, stays flat in 2023 and 2023BPix, rises by ~5% in 2024 and by 10-20% in 2025 and 2026 (up to ~30% in the H sideband in 2026). The pre-fit band of these cards (lumi, cross sections, MC statistics, no shape systematics) is ~1%: something in DY or EWK Z is mismodelled exactly in the VBF-like topology where the DNN puts the signal, and it is not the same in every era.

    4. The EWK Z fit with the VBF selector¶

    The measurement goes where the Higgs is absent: the Z sideband and the H sideband, which share the VBF selection with the SR but not its m(mumu) window (H sideband in 2024: 0.6 VBF H + ggH events against 199 EWK Z). The variable is the VBF selector, a classifier trained to separate EWK Z from DY in that topology (studies/vbf_selector/), so that the fit can tell the two apart, which the DNN cannot. EWK Z is the signal of this fit (r = EWK Z yield / prediction); the DY gets free normalisations per channel, split in hard and pile-up jets (hard-pu), with the alternatives single (one DY template) and hard-pu-lnn (DY constrained by the 012J fit).

    Results so far, without the shape systematics (luminosity, cross sections, free DY, MC statistics; combine/results_fit_matrix/SUMMARY.md):

    DY model expected precision on r (all eras) r on data (all eras) r on data per era
    hard-pu ±0.032 (stat ±0.013) 1.05 ± 0.03 0.62-1.27
    hard-pu-lnn ±0.030 1.12 ± 0.03 0.52-1.48
    single ±0.031 0.80 ± 0.03 0.28-1.51
    • The precision is there: ±3% on the EWK Z normalisation would cost the DNN fit almost nothing (between the 2.14 of ±0.5% and the 2.07 of ±10%), while leaving it free costs 23%.
    • The eras do not agree (r from 0.5 in 2022 to 1.3-1.5 in 2024/2026, far beyond the errors), and the DY model matters: a single DY template cannot follow the data in 2025 and the fit runs away (r = 0.28 with the luminosity nuisance at its boundary), while the hard/pile-up split describes the same data within 1%. This is a statement about the modelling, and it is exactly what the DNN fit would inherit silently.

    5. What comes next¶

    1. EWK Z fit with every shape systematic (JES, JER, muon scale and resolution re-evaluate the selector score event by event): production of the shifted templates is being completed, then studies/vbf_selector/fit_ewkz_systematics.py and combine/run_fit_matrix.sh vbfsel. The question is whether the era spread is absorbed by the systematics or is a real mismodelling.
    2. Carry the measurement into the DNN fit: an EWK Z normalisation common to SR and H sideband, either constrained by the sideband result (lnN with its error) or fitted together with the Z and H sideband channels of the selector in one combined card.
    3. Fix what the DNN cards miss: no normalisation uncertainty at all on TT, ST, TW, VV, VVV, W, TTX, SingleH (their cross sections are not found in crossSections13p6TeV.yaml by process name); the VBF-selector fit already builds them per source.
